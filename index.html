PART 01 / LECTURE & DEMONSTRATIONS

What is digital media?

A medium is a means of expression or communication. Paint, paper and air carrying sound are examples of physical media. Digital media represents information as discrete values that computers can store, process and transmit, usually as binary data. It does not have to be encrypted or displayed on a screen.

Capture

Camera samples light; microphone samples sound.

Process

Edit, encode, resize or compress the data.

Deliver

Store files or transmit them to a browser.
Discuss · 5 min

Follow a smartphone photo from capture to a social media post. Where might its dimensions, metadata or compression change?

From analog to digital

A printed photo reflects light continuously across its surface; a digital photo stores numerical samples in a grid. A bit is one binary digit (0 or 1); eight bits form a byte. Text, sound and images all use binary representations, but their file structures differ.

Example: a camera samples light, converts sensor readings to numbers and encodes the result as RAW or a processed image. Encryption is optional and unrelated to whether media is digital.

Capture

Sensor or input creates samples.

Represent

Pixels, paths, samples or frames.

Deliver

Encoded file or stream for a specific audience.

Raster versus vector

Raster images store a grid of pixels. Vector artwork describes shapes with paths, coordinates and fills. Vector shapes can be rendered at different sizes without enlarging a fixed pixel grid; embedded raster images do not gain this advantage.

Zoom experiment

Increase zoom. Which edge stays smooth? Both ultimately appear on a pixel-based screen.

SVG is an XML-based web format ideal for logos, icons and diagrams. Inline SVG can be styled or scripted. EPS is a legacy print/exchange format, not a native browser image; it may contain raster content.

Mini task · 10 min

Create a simple logo in Illustrator. Export SVG and a 64-pixel PNG. Place both at 256 pixels wide and compare edges and file sizes.

Choose the right image format

Lossless compression reconstructs the encoded pixel data exactly. Lossy compression discards information to reduce size. Reducing a photo to a GIF palette can lose colors even though GIF's subsequent compression is lossless. File size depends on content, dimensions and encoder settings.

PART 01 / LECTURE & DEMONSTRATIONS

Resolution is not just PPI

Pixel dimensions describe how many samples a raster contains. PPI describes pixel density at a physical size (especially print); DPI describes printer dots. A 1920 × 1080 image has 2,073,600 pixels regardless of its PPI metadata. At 300 PPI it prints at 6.4 × 3.6 inches without resampling; at 72 PPI it prints larger. Resampling changes the pixel grid. Web layout uses CSS pixels and device pixel ratio, not a universal 72 PPI rule.

Pixel & print calculator

Raw size = width × height × bits per pixel ÷ 8. This excludes file headers, padding and compression.

Photoshop experiment · 10 min

Open Image Size. Disable Resample and change 72 to 300 PPI. Record pixel dimensions and print size. Enable Resample and repeat with a fixed print size. Explain why the web cares about pixel dimensions.

Color, channels and bit depth

RGB mixes light additively; CMYK inks subtract portions of reflected light. Real ink and paper require device profiles; simple equations are only approximations. A color model describes components; a color space defines their meaning and gamut. sRGB is a dependable web default, while Display P3 and Adobe RGB cover different, larger regions. Gamut and bit depth are separate: 8-bit RGB offers about 16.7 million combinations, not a guarantee of any gamut coverage. HDR involves luminance range and transfer functions, not just more bits.

RGB mixer

Try all zero, all 255, and red plus green.

Bit depth and banding

For RGB, 8 bits per channel × 3 channels = 24 bits per pixel. Adding an 8-bit alpha channel gives 32 bits per pixel. Higher bit depth can reduce banding during edits.

Alpha transparency

Alpha
Practice · 10 min

Export a logo with a soft shadow as PNG and JPEG. Place them on dark and light backgrounds. Explain why a white background is not transparency.

Color management in production

RGB and CMYK are models. sRGB, Display P3 and Adobe RGB describe different color spaces. A profile helps software interpret the stored numbers. Assigning a profile changes their interpretation; converting profiles changes the numbers to preserve appearance where possible. Export with the intended profile, usually sRGB for general web delivery. CMYK printing depends on ink, paper and printer profiles.

At 8 bits per channel, RGB has 256³ = 16,777,216 possible code combinations. That does not prove a screen can display all of them accurately. A wide gamut is not the same as high bit depth or HDR.

Responsive images

A 600 CSS-pixel wide image on a device with a pixel ratio of 2 may benefit from a 1200-pixel source. Sending a huge image to every device wastes bandwidth. Use multiple source widths with srcset and sizes; reserve space with width and height. Use alternative text for informative images and empty alt text for decorative ones.

PART 01 / LECTURE & DEMONSTRATIONS

An advertisement is a communication problem

A display ad appears alongside other content; a social feed ad competes with posts; a story or reel fills a tall screen. The goal is not to fill the canvas but to communicate one clear message to a particular audience.

Audience

Who should care? What problem do they have?

Offer

What is the value? Why now?

CTA

What action should they take?

Example: A fictional coffee shop targets commuters with “Order ahead, skip the line.” A strong product shot, short headline and “Order now” outperform a cluttered block of competing text in a small placement.

Visual hierarchy and composition

Size, weight, contrast, position and negative space guide the eye. Alignment connects elements; repetition reinforces brand identity. Use one dominant focal point, one main message and a legible call to action. Test the ad at actual display size, not just zoomed in.

Compare two layouts

Which has a clearer first focal point? Which message survives at thumbnail size? Explain your reasoning.

Aspect ratio is not resolution

Aspect ratio expresses the relationship of width to height. 1080 × 1920 and 720 × 1280 are both 9:16. Cropping removes content, fitting can add empty space, and stretching distorts shapes. Recomposition means repositioning elements to fit the new canvas.

Camera language and continuity

PART 01 / LECTURE & DEMONSTRATIONS

Video: frames, time and compression

Video presents frames over time, commonly at 24, 25, 30, 50 or 60 fps (including fractional variants). At 30 fps a minute contains 1,800 displayed frames. Encoding represents media in a defined format; it often compresses it. Decoding reconstructs playable samples. Transcoding decodes and re-encodes, whereas remuxing repackages encoded streams without re-encoding.

Frame-rate simulator

Motion is sampled at the selected rate; the display refresh rate also limits what you see. This is a simulation, not an encoded video.

How inter-frame compression works

I frame

Intra-coded picture; uses information within the picture.

P frame

Prediction from reference pictures plus residual differences.

B frame

May use prediction from earlier and later pictures.

Encoders exploit spatial similarity and changes across time; they do not simply delete duplicate images. Not every codec uses the same frame structure. Motion, texture and noise can require more data to preserve quality.

Video storage estimator

Estimated decimal MB = total Mbps × seconds ÷ 8. Actual VBR output and container overhead vary.

Codecs are not containers

A codec defines how media is encoded and decoded. A container organizes streams, timing, captions and metadata. An MP4 extension does not tell you which video codec is inside.

Video codec Typical containers Production decision
H.264 / AVC MP4, MOV Reliable general delivery
H.265 / HEVC MP4, MOV Efficient high-resolution delivery; support varies
VP9 WebM, MKV Web streaming
AV1 WebM, MP4, MKV Efficient delivery; encoding and decoding costs vary
ProRes / DNxHR MOV, MXF (depending on codec) Editing intermediates, not default web delivery

AVI, FLV and Windows Media are historical formats; DivX and MPEG-4 Part 2 are legacy codecs. MPEG-4 is a family of standards, not a single codec. MP4/H.264/AAC is a practical baseline; WebM/VP9/Opus is another option. Check target devices and codec profiles.

Digital audio

Sample rate is samples per second per channel; bit depth is precision per PCM sample; channels describe separate streams such as stereo. 44.1 kHz is common for music and 48 kHz for video. WAV often contains uncompressed PCM; FLAC is lossless; MP3, AAC and Opus are typically lossy. Bitrate is data per second, not sample rate.

Sample a waveform

The dots illustrate discrete sampling, not the reconstructed analog waveform. At least more than twice the highest frequency is needed for ideal band-limited reconstruction.

Practice · 10 min

HDR, SDR and color management

SDR commonly uses Rec.709 for video delivery. HDR can use PQ or HLG transfer functions and wider color primaries; bit depth and gamut alone do not define HDR. An HDR clip interpreted with the wrong transfer function or output transform may appear washed out, dark or clipped. Tone mapping deliberately maps HDR luminance into the SDR range; it is not simply reducing exposure.

PART 01 / LECTURE & DEMONSTRATIONS

Story structure and pacing

A short reel needs a reason to keep watching. A hook establishes interest, the middle develops the idea, and the payoff resolves it. For an educational reel, state the question, demonstrate the answer, then summarize. For gameplay, establish the challenge, show progress, and end on a meaningful moment rather than random cuts.

Build a 60-second edit plan

Editing workflow in Premiere

  1. Create a project and organize footage into bins (video, audio, graphics, sequences).
  2. Import clips and inspect source properties. If high-resolution footage is slow, create proxies and toggle them for editing.
  3. Set the sequence frame size, timebase and color space; for the final use 1080 × 1920. Reframe horizontal footage rather than stretching.
  4. Mark in/out points, assemble a rough cut, then refine timing with trim, ripple edit and intentional cuts.
  5. Use Position and Scale keyframes for simple motion. Apply transitions only when they serve the story.
  6. Adjust audio clip gain and track levels; avoid clipping. Keep speech understandable over music. Fade music at the beginning and end.
  7. Save and review the edit. Export a test and watch it outside the editor.

Captions and accessibility

Captions represent spoken dialogue and meaningful non-speech sounds. Subtitles often translate dialogue. Automatic speech recognition is a starting point, not a finished accessibility deliverable. Correct names, punctuation and timing. Use readable contrast, sensible line breaks and enough display time. SRT and WebVTT store timed text; burn-in makes captions visible but not selectable.

PART 01 / LECTURE & DEMONSTRATIONS

Export decisions

Choose a container and codec the target accepts. MP4/H.264/AAC is a practical baseline for broad delivery; HEVC and AV1 can be efficient but may have workflow compatibility limits. Match frame rate to the intended sequence, avoid unnecessary upscaling, and adjust bitrate to the quality required. VBR allocates bits based on complexity; CBR targets a more constant rate. Export a short test before a full render.

Setting Practical baseline for this project
Frame size 1080 × 1920, vertical
Frame rate Match intended sequence / source cadence
Video codec + container H.264 / MP4
Audio AAC, 48 kHz, stereo when appropriate
Color SDR Rec.709 unless HDR is intentionally tested
Bitrate Start with a reasonable preset, then inspect quality and size
Captions Accurate visible captions, plus separate SRT when applicable

Social platforms can re-encode uploads. Uploading an HDR master does not guarantee immediate HDR playback. Test actual exports on the destination and avoid judging color from only one application.

Publish for real users

Choose dimensions based on displayed size and device density. Use responsive image candidates, meaningful alternative text, explicit dimensions to prevent layout shift, and lazy loading below the fold. Avoid lazy loading the main hero image. For video, supply controls, captions and a poster; avoid autoplay with sound. Use transcripts for audio. Preserve masters; publish optimized copies.
